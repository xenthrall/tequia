I don't really know how to start this, and I have to confess that writing this note makes me a bit nervous.
To begin with, I'd like to tell you that I'm preparing, learning and building things with the goal of becoming one of those people who contribute to innovation and, who knows, to creating some pretty crazy things :3.

## Who I am

Hi, I'm Jhon. I was born in El Espino, Boyacá, Colombia, on January 6, 2006. These days I work in software development, although getting here wasn't exactly a path I had planned from the start.

I've been pretty curious since I was a kid, and I've always liked learning by doing. During my teenage years I spent a lot of time helping my dad in his carpentry workshop. That's where I learned to work with wood and to use many of the tools we had there. Over time I also started experimenting on my own, and I ended up building some furniture for myself, several pieces of which I still have.

But the curiosity didn't stay in the workshop. I was also really drawn to electronics, computers and anything that could be taken apart to find out how it worked. My mom was the one who brought the first computers into our home. I don't remember the exact year, but around 2014 we had a Windows 7 desktop PC. That was my first computer and also one of my first places to experiment: I learned to take it apart, put it back together and, once or twice, I also managed to break a computer or a home appliance while trying to figure out how things worked.

Then came a Lenovo laptop that we shared at home. I started using it around 2016 and, to be honest, over time I pretty much claimed it as my own. It was one of the computers I experimented with the most during those years.

My curiosity also wandered down other paths. For a while I learned video editing and made AMVs, those music videos that mix songs with scenes from TV shows or anime. I also tried making cartoons, animations and other things that probably never went anywhere. In 2022 I even built a small video game about a character who had to dodge asteroids, following a Godot Engine course by Adderly Céspedes (Adderly2dd). Once it was done, I worked up the courage to publish it on itch.io. It got around ten downloads, haha, but for me what mattered was finishing it and putting it out there.

In 2021 a relative gave me another computer: an ASUS with an 11th-gen Intel Core i3. That machine ended up being with me through a pretty important stage. On it I took my first programming courses in 2022, studied for my technologist degree in Software Analysis and Development, and also did tech maintenance work. It was even the computer I used to work remotely at my first job as a developer.

While all that was happening, I kept learning other things too. Besides carpentry, my dad taught me some basic mechanics and how to ride a motorcycle. Since 2019 I've also been learning to play the piano. I'm by no means a musician, but I can already play a few pieces I like, and I really enjoy the process of learning something new.

I still have that ASUS that stayed with me for so many years. Even so, in September 2026 I decided to buy my current computer with my own savings: an ASUS Vivobook 16 with an Intel Core Ultra 5. There's something special to me about that purchase being possible after the whole road I traveled with those first computers.

I guess, looking back on all of this, software development didn't come out of nowhere. Before writing code, I already liked building things, taking them apart, modifying them and understanding how they worked. First it was furniture, then computers, the electronics experiments, the videos, the small games, and finally software.

I'm still pretty curious. The difference is that now I have better tools to turn some of those ideas into things that actually work.

## What I do

These days I work in software development. I like building tools that solve real problems, learning how things work and experimenting with new technologies.

I also keep a few personal projects where I can try out ideas more freely. Not all of them get finished, but almost all of them leave me with something new to learn.

## Why this space

For a long time, what I learn has ended up scattered across projects, notes and repositories. Some of it might be useful to someone else, so I decided to start writing it down.

I'm not planning to publish on any particular schedule. I want this to simply be a space to share ideas, lessons, projects and experiences whenever I have something worth telling.

## What you'll find here

You'll probably find notes about:

* Software development and technology.
* Personal projects and experiments.
* Things I learn along the way.
* Ideas, experiences and the occasional curiosity.

I don't claim to always have the right answer. Some notes will simply be a snapshot of what I was learning at the time.

### To wrap up

I guess this is a pretty long way of saying hi.

I'm still learning, building things and figuring out what I can do with all of this. So if you made it this far, thanks for reading.

See you in another note.
